As competition with the US for technological dominance intensifies, China is embarking on a multi-trillion-dollar infrastructure push aimed at laying the foundations for long-term growth in an era it expects to be shaped by artificial intelligence.  
随着中美科技主导权之争日益激烈，中国正启动一项价值数万亿美元的基础设施建设计划，旨在为人工智能时代预期的长期增长奠定基础。

Beijing is betting that success won’t solely depend on advanced chips and AI models, but also on the physical systems that support them — from power grids and data centers to telecommunications networks and logistics.  
中国政府认为，成功不仅取决于先进芯片和 AI 模型，更取决于支撑这些技术的实体系统——从电网、数据中心到通信网络和物流体系。

The initiative, known as the Six Networks, is part of Beijing’s latest Five-Year Plan. It marks the first time China has brought its biggest infrastructure projects under a single, integrated program, rather than developing transport, energy and communications networks separately.  
这项名为“六网”的倡议是北京最新五年规划的一部分。这标志着中国首次将其最大的基础设施项目整合到一个统一的计划中，而非分别发展交通、能源和通信网络。

As well as supporting the country’s AI ambitions, the government hopes the project will strengthen China’s manufacturing base, bolster resilience to geopolitical tensions, economic shocks and natural disasters, and advance the transition to a low-carbon economy.  
除了支持国家的人工智能雄心外，政府还希望该项目能强化中国的制造业基础，增强对地缘政治紧张局势、经济冲击和自然灾害的抵御能力，并推动向低碳经济转型。

What is the Six Networks?  
什么是"六网"？

The Six Networks is a national infrastructure program to upgrade China’s vast physical and digital systems. Scheduled for completion by 2030, it spans six areas: electricity, water, telecommunications, computing, logistics and underground pipe networks. [China Securities Co.](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/6066%20HK%20Equity) estimates the project will cost more than 26 trillion yuan ($3.8 trillion).  
"六网"是一项旨在升级中国庞大物理与数字系统的国家基础设施计划。该项目计划于 2030 年前完成，涵盖六大领域：电力、水利、通信、算力、物流及地下管网。中证公司预估该项目投资将超过 26 万亿元人民币（约合 3.8 万亿美元）。

A central focus is digital infrastructure. China plans to build high-performance supercomputing centers, general-purpose data centers and AI-focused compute hubs, as well as develop new ultra-high-speed fiber broadband, satellite internet and a sixth-generation, or 6G, mobile network.  
该计划的核心聚焦于数字基础设施建设。中国拟建设高性能超级计算中心、通用数据中心和人工智能专用算力枢纽，同时发展新型超高速光纤宽带、卫星互联网以及第六代（6G）移动通信网络。

The electricity grid is also to be upgraded. Beijing plans to expand long-distance transmission lines, strengthen urban and rural networks, and build more energy storage facilities to absorb renewable power. It also plans to develop an AI-powered dispatch system capable of monitoring the grid in real time, adjusting power flows and matching renewable energy supply with data center demand.  
电网也将进行升级改造。北京计划扩建远距离输电线路，强化城乡电网，并建设更多储能设施以消纳可再生能源电力。同时还将开发人工智能驱动的调度系统，实现电网实时监控、动态调整电力流向，并将可再生能源供应与数据中心需求精准匹配。

The project also includes an upgrade to transport and water infrastructure. Logistics networks are set to have improved connections between production sites, ports and distribution hubs. As part of the plan, existing flood-control systems — such as embankments, dykes and reservoirs — will be improved, while diversion projects that shift water from wet basins to arid areas and large irrigation districts will be expanded. Finally, the government plans to build 770,000 kilometers (478,460 miles) of water, drainage, gas and heating pipelines beneath Chinese cities to replace aging infrastructure, reduce leaks and flooding, and support urban development.  
该项目还包括对交通和水务基础设施的升级改造。物流网络将加强生产基地、港口和配送中心之间的互联互通。根据规划，现有的防洪系统——如堤坝、水库等——将得到改善，同时从丰水流域向干旱地区调水的引水工程及大型灌区也将进行扩建。最后，政府计划在中国城市地下新建 77 万公里（约 47.8 万英里）的供水、排水、燃气和供热管道，以替换老化设施、减少泄漏和洪涝风险，并支持城市发展。

Why is China investing so heavily in the Six Networks?  
中国为何大力投资"东数西算"工程？

Competition with the US over AI has made infrastructure a strategic priority for Beijing. As AI models become larger and more sophisticated, they require enormous amounts of computing power and electricity, cooling water, high-speed communications networks and efficient supply chains. Rather than treating those needs separately, China wants to build an integrated system.  
中美在人工智能领域的竞争使得基础设施成为北京的战略重点。随着 AI 模型变得更大更复杂，它们需要巨大的计算能力、电力、冷却水、高速通信网络和高效供应链。中国希望将这些需求整合起来，打造一个综合系统，而非各自为政。

Much of the attention has focused on the computing and telecommunications networks. The government wants to connect scattered data processing hubs across the country through high-speed, stable networks, creating a nationally coordinated [system](http://www.china.com.cn/zhibo/content_118445710.htm) that can direct AI workloads to whichever data centers have spare capacity — similar to how electricity flows through a power grid. Officials also expect the network to underpin a wide range of AI uses, from factory automation and self-driving cars to robots.  
大部分关注点集中在计算和通信网络上。政府希望通过高速稳定的网络连接全国分散的数据处理中心，形成全国协调的系统，能够将 AI 工作负载分配到有空闲能力的数据中心——类似于电力在电网中的流动方式。官员们还期待这一网络能支撑从工厂自动化、自动驾驶汽车到机器人等广泛的 AI 应用场景。

Electricity is another piece of the puzzle. New transmission lines and renewable energy projects are intended to provide abundant, low-cost clean power to data centers, whose power consumption [is almost the equivalent](https://www.cac.gov.cn/2026-07/15/c_1785861481530197.htm) to twice the annual output of the Three Gorges Dam — the world’s largest hydropower project by installed capacity. Expanding the adoption of renewable energy would also advance Beijing’s carbon-neutral goals while bolstering energy security.  
电力是这一宏大计划的另一关键环节。新建输电线路和可再生能源项目旨在为数据中心提供充足、低成本的清洁电力——这些数据中心的耗电量几乎相当于三峡大坝年发电量的两倍（三峡是全球装机容量最大的水电工程）。扩大可再生能源应用既能推进北京实现碳中和目标，又能增强能源安全。

Other parts of the Six Networks plan support broader economic priorities. Improvements to the logistics network are designed to reduce transport costs and better connect demand in China’s large, populous cities with supply from the farthest mountainous areas. The water initiatives are aimed at strengthening food security and resilience against floods and droughts. The hope is that modernizing underground pipe networks should improve living conditions and also create construction work at a time when China is in the midst of a property downturn.  
"六网"计划的其他部分支持更广泛的经济优先事项。物流网络的改进旨在降低运输成本，更好地将中国人口众多的大城市需求与偏远山区供应连接起来。水利举措则着眼于增强粮食安全及抵御洪旱灾害的能力。在地下管网现代化方面，期望能改善生活条件，同时在中国房地产低迷时期创造建筑就业机会。

How will China pay for the Six Networks?  
中国将如何为"六网"计划提供资金？

The cost of the project is expected to be nearly 50% more than the combined investment across those six sectors during the last Five-Year Plan from 2021 to 2025, according to [estimates](https://mp.weixin.qq.com/s/Zzq16g_zX4FBZHDqT333fw) by China Securities Co.  
根据中国证券公司的估算，该项目成本预计将比 2021 至 2025 年上一个五年计划期间这六个领域的总投资高出近 50%。

Public-interest infrastructure, such as flood control systems and water diversion initiatives, is expected to be funded mainly through the issuance of central and local government bonds, alongside lending from policy banks — state-owned lenders that finance projects aligned with Beijing’s strategic priorities.

Revenue-generating projects, including logistics hubs, are expected to attract private capital through public-private partnerships. For underground pipelines, for example, investors may be [granted operating concession rights](https://www.ndrc.gov.cn/wsdwhfz/202607/t20260703_1406277.html) that enable them to earn recurring revenue after the projects are completed.

More commercially viable projects — including power, computing and communication networks — are expected to rely primarily on market-based corporate investment.

Can China really deliver this?

China has a strong track record of delivering major projects under its five-year plans. Large amounts of public money are set aside every year for such initiatives and approvals for land and other resources are often fast-tracked. Local officials also have strong incentives to ensure projects are completed, as their performance reviews and promotion prospects can be tied to how quickly and effectively they deliver them.

That said, financing could be a challenge this time. Economic growth has [slowed](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/CNGDPYOY%20Index) as consumer spending remains weak amid a soft job market and a prolonged real estate slump, weighing on corporate profits and investment. Local governments, which have traditionally shouldered much of the cost of infrastructure spending, have also seen income from [land sales](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/CHGRSLRR%20Index) shrink to less than half of its 2021 peak. At the same time, Beijing has tightened limits on their borrowing as it tries to [contain risks](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-09/china-gets-chance-at-fiscal-rethink-after-debt-cleanup-milestone) from the huge stockpile of off-balance-sheet debt that has accumulated since the Global Financial Crisis.

Technology restrictions could also complicate the project. US-led export controls have [limited China’s access](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-14/small-amount-of-nvidia-ai-chips-shipped-to-china-with-us-license) to cutting-edge semiconductors, potentially slowing progress on the computing and communications networks at the heart of the project.

Another challenge will be the bureaucracy and coordination required for a project that spans multiple sectors. Government researchers have [warned](https://www.toutiao.com/article/7662942326834135592/?&amp;source=m_redirect) that isolated planning across different government departments, inconsistent technical standards, and unclear benefit-sharing rules could lead to overlapping investment and undermine the project’s success.

Is the US also ramping up its AI infrastructure?

Silicon Valley is betting that physical infrastructure is key to building more advanced AI systems and keeping ahead of China in the race for AI dominance.

Top US tech firms, including Meta Platforms Inc., Alphabet Inc.’s Google and Amazon.com Inc., plan to spend [hundreds of billions of dollars](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-02-06/how-much-is-big-tech-spending-on-ai-computing-a-staggering-650-billion-in-2026) to build sprawling new data centers stocked with cutting-edge AI chips. OpenAI’s Sam Altman has [previously said](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-08-15/openai-s-altman-expects-to-spend-trillions-on-infrastructure) he expects his firm to spend “trillions” on infrastructure to develop and run AI.

The US government has thrown its support behind this push. The Trump administration has previously unveiled plans to [use federal land](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-04-03/us-energy-chief-plans-to-use-federal-land-to-build-data-centers) and resources to build data centers, and steps to [ensure there is enough power](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-10-24/trump-administration-moves-to-speed-data-center-power-hookups) to run energy-hungry AI data centers. Not all policymakers are on board, however. A growing number of local and state officials have taken tougher stances on data center development, following a surge in community pushback over the impacts on utility prices and the environment.

New York Governor Kathy Hochul recently announced a one-year moratorium on new large data center projects in the state. President Donald Trump responded by calling it a “terrible decision.” “Data Centers are tremendous WINS for the States and Communities that are lucky enough to get them,” he wrote in a social media post.
